**Faunapoolen — a new sales story**

16 September 2026 · Approved direction, implemented in local development. This replaces the previous copy report. The blog is excluded.

**The mistake in the first report**

I treated the existing pages as the starting point and improved their sentences. That preserved the old sales argument. Leading with chlorine-free swimming still made a technical attribute carry the emotional reason to buy.

This proposal starts with the customer's desired outcome and works back to the offer, evidence and first contact. Existing headings, sections and package names have no special status. The constraints are the real services, truthful claims, the framework and your established design decisions—including three package blocks beside each other.

**The commercial direction**

Build the story around making the garden a place people want to spend more of their life: somewhere to swim, gather, unwind and enjoy being home.

A nature pool makes that possibility tangible. Faunapoolen's role is to help the customer turn it into a considered design and a finished installation they understand how to care for.

Our working audience is a homeowner considering a substantial improvement to their property. They may already want water but be uncertain about the form, investment or work involved. This is a positioning hypothesis, not a claim that we have conducted customer research. Brita's account of enjoying the pool with her grandchildren gives us a real example of the desired outcome; one account does not establish everyone's motivation.

The site should produce **qualified conversations that can become suitable projects**. Price visibility, honest care expectations and clear service scope belong in that objective. More form submissions alone would be an inadequate measure of success.

**The StoryBrand argument**

Miller's useful discipline here is to connect the customer's desire, obstacle, guide, plan and action to a recognisable outcome. The guide earns confidence through understanding and evidence. The table below is an internal writing brief; it is not seven blocks to publish. [Donald Miller, _Your Brand Is Not the Hero_](https://storybrand.com/downloads/your-brand-is-not-the-hero.pdf).

| Story element           | Our proposed argument                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What the customer wants | A garden that becomes a favourite part of everyday life.                                                                                                                 |
| External problem        | They need to turn an idea into something suitable for their property and budget.                                                                                         |
| Internal problem        | They cannot yet judge whether the result will justify the investment and responsibility.                                                                                 |
| Underlying belief       | The garden should serve the way they want to live at home.                                                                                                               |
| Guide                   | We help them make the decisions, design and build the water feature, and understand its care.                                                                            |
| Plan                    | Explore the possibilities → agree the proposal → build and hand over.                                                                                                    |
| Action                  | Request an initial conversation with a clear purpose.                                                                                                                    |
| Success                 | A finished place they enjoy using, with realistic ownership expectations.                                                                                                |
| Stakes                  | Spending heavily on something that disappoints, dominates the garden or demands care they did not anticipate. Explain the relevant decisions without inventing a crisis. |

The most important change is the order of persuasion: **desire → belief → personal relevance → confidence → contact**. Filtration, materials and certification support that progression when they answer a question.

**The proposed homepage opening**

Swedish working copy:

> **Gör trädgården till hemmets bästa plats.**
>
> Vi utformar och bygger naturpooler, dammar och bäckar för morgondopp, långa sommarkvällar och mer tid tillsammans.
>
> **Be om rådgivning**
>
> Första telefonsamtalet är kostnadsfritt.
>
> Se en färdig naturpool →

English direction:

> **Make your garden the best part of being home.**
>
> We design and build natural pools, ponds and streams for morning swims, long summer evenings and time together.
>
> **Request a consultation**
>
> Your first phone consultation is free.
>
> See a completed natural pool →

This is a recommended creative direction, not a claim of proven conversion performance. The headline gives the improvement a personal purpose. The supporting sentence immediately identifies the services and our role. The invitation offers help with a decision. The secondary link supplies evidence for visitors who need to believe the promise before contacting us.

The headline needs the concrete service sentence beneath it. Without that sentence, it could belong to almost any garden business. Do not add another slogan, introductory paragraph or image caption.

**What the rest of the homepage must do**

1. **Make the outcome believable.** Follow the opening with the completed Gotland project and Brita's actual account of using the pool. Keep the link direct: “Se naturpoolen på Gotland.” Show what we have built and what it means to its owner. Decorative concept imagery cannot do the same job as evidence.
2. **Help the visitor recognise their own situation.** A short invitation can address uncertainty without assuming distress: “Du behöver inte ha en färdig plan.” Follow it with the substance: “Vi hjälper dig att hitta en lösning utifrån hur du vill använda trädgården, tomtens förutsättningar och din budget.” This earns its space by making an early conversation possible.
3. **Give the investment a shape.** Present the three pool options together, with confirmed starting prices, swimming areas and meaningful differences. A visitor should be able to judge whether this is the right scale of purchase. People interested in ponds or streams need a clear route to their own offer.
4. **Make the route to ownership understandable.** Three distinct stages: “Ett första samtal / Förslag och offert / Anläggning och genomgång.” Explain the paid site assessment where it becomes relevant. The plan should show what the customer gains at each stage, rather than describe our internal workflow.
5. **Give the final invitation a reason.** Use “Ta reda på vad som passar din trädgård.” The first conversation should help establish the desired use, broad possibilities and sensible next step. Ask for the contact, then stop writing.

This is a proposed sequence, not a demand for five additional sections. Combine or remove existing sections to achieve it. Every paragraph must create desire, provide evidence, resolve a decision or make the next step clear.

**Nature pools: sell the experience and the decision**

A possible opening is **“Ditt eget badställe, precis utanför dörren.”** The page must then earn the idea with information that a serious buyer can use.

The main questions are: How would we use it? Will it suit our property? What is the likely investment? What will we have to look after? Put answers beside the relevant choices. Do not hide all practical information in the FAQ or turn the whole page into a technical manual.

“Biological filtration” belongs where the buyer asks how the water is maintained. Explain it simply and connect it to the actual system and care routine. Chlorine-free operation is a supporting product benefit. Avoid implying that biological filtration removes the owner's responsibilities.

**Packages: three ways to judge the investment**

Keep the three adjacent blocks, but write them to help customers compare the scale and scope of the project.

For each block, use a clear swimming area, confirmed starting price, one use-oriented line and comparable inclusions. Possible use lines are “För dagliga dopp”, “För bad tillsammans” and “För mer plats vid vattnet”, subject to the actual designs. These can replace existing decorative names and descriptions; they should not become an extra layer of text.

The comparison must distinguish design work from installed features. Show what is common once, then the differences. Explain total space requirements separately from swimming area. A reader must not infer that filtration, planting or construction is absent merely because it is omitted from one card.

Use **“Fråga om den här poolen”** to carry their interest into the conversation. Selecting a card should not ask them to finalise a design.

The recorded prices and additions still need commercial confirmation. Until then, the report treats them as indicative figures. Confirm scope before rewriting the offer around them; persuasive precision must reflect something we can deliver.

**Waterscapes: give this audience its own reason to act**

A pond or stream buyer may have no interest in swimming. Their story can centre on a place to pause, watch and spend time outdoors.

Lead with that use, name the installations clearly and help them choose through concrete differences. The copy should answer “What would I enjoy here?” and “What would caring for it involve?” A photograph can show stone and planting; the words should help the visitor decide whether fish, flowing water or a still surface fits the experience they want.

Give these visitors an enquiry about their garden rather than a preselected swimming-pool package. Do not let the pool's prices or maintenance expectations appear to apply to every water feature.

**Trust: show the guide doing the job**

Suggested About passage:

> **Du ska inte behöva bli poolexpert för att få en bra pool.**
>
> Vi hjälper dig att förstå valen, planerar lösningen för din tomt och bygger den. När arbetet är klart visar vi hur du tar hand om anläggningen.

Its purpose is to express the customer's burden and our responsibility together. Follow it with evidence: the completed project, Brita's account of the build and the actual certification. Avoid unsupported claims about years of experience, project counts, exceptional results or being the best.

The two Brita quotations are one customer's evidence, not two independent customer stories. Preserve her wording and attribution. Additional case facts—brief, dimensions, timeline, budget and challenges—must come from the project record or customer, not be inferred from photographs.

Keep the company voice as **we/us**. The customer needs confidence in the relationship and delivery; a named founder story is unnecessary.

**FAQ: organise around decisions, not service inventory**

Start with these questions, phrased naturally in each language:

| Decision                                   | What the answer needs to establish                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Could this work in our garden?             | What we assess and how the customer begins. No blanket promise of suitability.                                                                 |
| How much space does a pool need?           | Swimming area versus the full installation, with verified requirements. A small fountain is not an answer to a pool-space question.            |
| What should we budget?                     | Confirmed starting prices, included scope and the main reasons the quotation changes.                                                          |
| How much care will it need?                | Owner tasks, seasonal work and what paid servicing covers. Give time estimates only when supported.                                            |
| Do we need a design before contacting you? | No finished design or package choice is needed to start a conversation.                                                                        |
| What happens after the first call?         | The agreed next step and when a paid site visit becomes relevant.                                                                              |
| What does a consultation cost?             | First phone call free; site visits paid, with the fee credited against installation if the customer proceeds, according to the current policy. |
| Can you help with an existing pond?        | The actual assessment, maintenance and repair services, with an appropriate contact route.                                                     |

Retain useful answers about winter, service area and customisation. Place them where they help the decision. Delete filler introductions to answers; a direct answer is often enough.

**The conversion offer needs to be stronger than “send us information”**

Recommended invitation:

> **Ta reda på vad som passar din trädgård.**
>
> I ett första telefonsamtal pratar vi om dina idéer, förutsättningarna och hur du kan gå vidare. Samtalet är kostnadsfritt.
>
> **Be om rådgivning**

This describes a useful exchange. It does not promise a free design, a completed site assessment or a quotation from a short call.

The contact page should continue that same offer. The initial information should be name, email, location and an optional short description, with phone available for arranging a call. A selected package can carry through as an interest. Site classifications, expanded sizes, additions and annual-care choices should not dominate the first-contact experience. That is a recommended flow change alongside the copy, not something already implemented.

Use **“Skicka förfrågan”** on submission. A successful message can say **“Tack. Vi kontaktar dig för att prata om din trädgård och nästa steg.”** Add a response-time promise only after the business commits to it. Error states should preserve the enquiry and give an available recovery; they need no sales language.

Keep the repeated footer invitation short. Functional navigation, language choices and contact details need familiar labels, not creative rewriting. The footer needs no additional manifesto.

**What must disappear from the writing**

- Repeated promises to adapt everything, without explaining a relevant decision.
- Poetic paragraphs whose only job is to describe the scene already visible.
- Technical detail introduced before the customer has a reason to care about it.
- Several versions of the same invitation stacked above one button.
- Instructions that make a visitor feel they must prepare a specification to speak to us.
- Invented urgency, guarantees, free deliverables or unsupported superiority.

**How we should judge this direction**

Before implementation, read the whole journey in order. Can someone identify the service immediately, want the outcome, see credible evidence, understand the scale of investment and know what the first contact gives them? Does each additional line advance one of those decisions?

After launch, assess qualified enquiries, progression to consultations and proposals, and enquiry abandonment. Customer questions should reveal where the story remains unclear. Use those findings to sharpen the message and the offer.

The next writing stage is a complete draft of the sales pages using this argument. It should replace existing copy wherever needed, with the confirmed commercial facts and real customer evidence as its foundation.
